## 1. What this report measures

For each prediction interval (50%, 80%, 90%), the **empirical coverage** is the fraction of half-hours in which the actual underlying demand fell inside the interval. A well-calibrated interval should have coverage close to its nominal level.

Confidence intervals (95%) use a **block bootstrap by calendar day** (1,000 resamples, seed 42). Half-hours within a day are always resampled together because forecast errors are strongly correlated within a day.

**Periods:**
- 2023: validation period (monthly expanding-window refits from 2009).
- 2024-25: test period (8 Mar 2024 – 31 Dec 2025, one-shot unsealing per D-023).

**Cold-day definition:**
- Both periods: 'cold' = daily GB mean *observed* temperature < 3 °C.
- 2024-25 only: 'cold' also computed from the archived ~48 h-ahead forecast temperature (`previous_day2`, as the operator would have known it). For 2023, only observed temperature is available; this is labelled clearly throughout.

## 7. Remaining uncertainty

- Cold-day classification uses a GB mean temperature across six equal-weight sites (D-012); population weighting is deferred.
- Bank holidays follow England & Wales rules; Scottish differences are a known simplification (D-021).
- Christmas period: 24 Dec – 2 Jan (from `calendar.py`).
- 'Top-5% demand days' threshold is computed within each evaluation period separately, not from a fixed training window.
- The 2024-25 test period starts 2024-03-08 (forecast radiation archive); year 2024 therefore covers March–December only.
- Bootstrap CIs assume independent blocks (days). Demand is seasonally correlated across days, so CIs may be slightly narrower than a fully block-aware method would give.
- No 2026 data was accessed in this analysis.
